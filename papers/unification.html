The Unification of Matter, Forces, and Spacetime from the Bost–Connes System

ShaoWu Hu · Independent Researcher, Shanghai, China
First published July 6, 2026 · Preprint
Version 7 · Updated October 15, 2026

Abstract

Starting from a finite p-adic reduction of the Bost–Connes system, we investigate the common dynamical origin of matter, mass, and forces. Matter is characterized as stable excitations whose patterns are preserved under propagation and exchange: the relative phases of their persistent evolution yield mass frequencies, exchange between stable states produces resonance responses, and the influence of the background energy distribution on motion produces a metric response.

We construct the complete process from the formation of a stable pattern to its persistent evolution, bringing path spectral responses, node overlaps, and distributions across generations into a single mass generator. The same mass parameter then governs both the rest-frame energy gap and the inertial response at low momentum, while a common background response provides a unified energy scale.

On the basis of these dynamical relations, we use an odd prime p as a structural parameter to establish evaluation relations for mass spectra and coupling constants. With p=7 and the common energy calibration E0=1 GeV, we obtain an electroweak scale of 246.19 GeV and an inverse electromagnetic coupling of αEM−1=137.03636. The absolute relative deviations of the nine charged-fermion masses from the reference central values are all below 1.5%, and those of the three charged leptons are below 0.003%. The neutrino spectrum predicts absolute masses of 5.07, 9.96, and 50.63 meV for the three mass eigenstates, with a mass-squared difference ratio of Δm312/Δm212=34.52.
