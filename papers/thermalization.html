The Mechanism of Thermalization in the Bost–Connes System

Shao-Wu Hu · Independent Researcher, Shanghai, China
August 5, 2026 · Preprint (V14)

Abstract

Thermalization brings matter toward equilibrium through sustained exchange, while information about the exchange processes remains in the full joint state. Starting from the dynamical cycle established on a finite reduction of the Bost–Connes system, we study the relation between thermal equilibrium, relaxation rates, and local irreversibility. At a fixed finite temperature, with independently refreshed environments, positive exchange shares, and same-temperature restoration as specified here, the full Gibbs state is the unique attracting state of the cycle. The occupation spectrum and coherence factors determine the relaxation rates of different observables and yield a controlled weak-exchange continuous limit on the restored family of states. Energy changes include both thermal exchange and background restoration. This gives conditions for tracking thermal states as the temperature changes, together with the late-time cooling law and its asymptotic corrections for a specified zero-temperature exit process.

The same joint realization determines the environment outputs and entropy balance. The decrease in system relative entropy characterizes the approach to equilibrium, while changes in auxiliary preparations enter the complete correlation and energy balance. The auxiliaries can continue to change even when the system is at equilibrium. We also obtain a return process in which the same interaction acts again on previously used auxiliaries. The endpoint, rate, and time direction of thermalization are thus determined by the equilibrium conditions, actual decay modes, and environmental boundary conditions of one process. They can be tested jointly through occupation, coherence, and energy-flow readings constrained by common parameters.
