The Mechanism of Thermalization in the Bost–Connes System

Shao-Wu Hu · Independent Researcher, Shanghai, China
August 5, 2026 · Preprint (V15)

Abstract

Thermalization brings matter toward equilibrium through sustained exchange, while exchange information remains in the joint state. Starting from the dynamical cycle on a finite reduction of the Bost–Connes system, we study the common mechanism underlying thermal equilibrium, relaxation, and local irreversibility. For the cycle constructed here, with independent environment refreshing and same-temperature restoration, we prove that the full Gibbs state is the unique attracting state at fixed finite positive temperature. The occupation spectrum and coherence factors determine observable relaxation rates, and a controlled weak-exchange limit gives continuous relaxation equations on the restored family of states. Thermal exchange and background restoration jointly determine energy changes, yielding conditions for thermal-state tracking and the late-time cooling law of a specified zero-temperature exit process.

Target exchange on complete carriers and an isospectral thermal reference provide a physical realization of this cycle, with a conditional swap and branch pointer implementing background restoration. When the realization preserves the carrier structure, finite switching incorporates maintenance into a driven cycle and control work balances changes in activity energy. If maintenance commutes with shell energy, the same Gibbs state remains attracting, and relaxation is governed by the complete driven-cycle spectrum.

Environment outputs also determine the entropy balance. Decreasing system relative entropy characterizes relaxation, while changes in auxiliary preparations account for environment energy and correlations; restoration auxiliaries may change even at system equilibrium. Reintroducing used auxiliaries can produce returns under the same joint dynamics. Joint tests of occupations, coherences, and energy flows therefore constrain the endpoint, rate, and environmental conditions of thermalization.
