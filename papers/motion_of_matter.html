The Mechanism of the Motion of Matter from the Bost–Connes System

Shao-Wu Hu · Shanghai, China
August 18, 2026 · Preprint (V8)

Abstract

We investigate how the same matter changes position while undergoing continual maintenance, using the dynamics of a finite Bost–Connes state space. Maintenance preserves the internal structure of matter, while position amplitudes and relative phases evolve under the joint action, with local position currents composing the propagation of the wave packet as a whole. In the uniform propagation model used here, with time calibrated by the total elapsed duration, the internal mass action determines both the rest energy and the curvature of the propagation spectrum. The long-wavelength, low-momentum expansion of a massive branch gives Minc02 = E0, linking the continual maintenance of matter to its inertial response.

The observable manifestations of motion further depend on the records formed during propagation. The double-slit cross term is controlled by the overlap of the complete unread outputs of the two paths; interference in the unconditioned screen distribution disappears when orthogonal records preserve the path distinction. Surviving matter continues to propagate from the conditional state following a record. When a composite object's collective branch is preserved, its conditional position distribution remains concentrated, and spreading and recoil are controlled, the low-speed equation for its center, together with successive records, gives an approximate description of a macroscopic trajectory. The maintenance of matter, coherent quantum propagation, and macroscopic motion are thus connected within the same dynamics.
