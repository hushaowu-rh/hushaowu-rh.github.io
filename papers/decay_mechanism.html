The Mechanism of Decay from the Bost–Connes System

Shao-Wu Hu · Shanghai, China
September 4, 2026 · Preprint (V3)

Abstract

Starting from the dynamics of the Bost–Connes state space and the identification of matter within it, we place maintenance of the original particle, transfer into multiparticle final states, and product propagation within one joint action. The parent and product amplitudes evolve together under this action. Distinguishable records retained by the products then specify the probability balance of uninterrupted survival and first decay. Summing first-decay probabilities over all rounds within each channel gives its branching fraction, while weighting the actual elapsed durations by survival probabilities gives the mean lifetime. In the narrow-resonance regime formed by weak coupling and a smooth outgoing spectrum, the same transfer coupling also gives an energy width, relating mean lifetime to spectral width by T̄cont = ℏ/Γ.

With explicitly specified effective vertices and response matching, product energy sharing, chiral contractions, and barrier propagation yield continuous spectra, helicity suppression, and exponential lifetime hierarchies, respectively. Long lifetime corresponds to suppressed open exits, while stability requires closure of all relevant exit actions. Why the original particle persists, how it transforms, and what spectra and records its products leave thus become related, calculable results within the same dynamical process.
