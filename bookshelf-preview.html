Axioms of Quantum Mechanics and the Arithmetic Structure of the Bost–Connes System

The five axioms of von Neumann quantum mechanics are each confirmed by experiment, yet they are not described within a single mathematical framework. Starting from the Bost–Connes system, we perform the p-adic reduction at a prime p and construct the state space H_p = L²(ℤ/p^Kℤ), K = p(p−1)−1, and argue that its structure naturally describes these five axioms. (I) C*-positivity gives the Hilbert space through the GNS construction, (II) invariance under Galois conjugation gives the self-adjoint observables, (III) the CRT decomposition gives the tensor product, and (IV) H = log n generates the unique unitary time evolution. (V) The Born rule comes from the modular structure S = JΔ^{1/2} of the KMS state, its probability weights being the squared moduli of the values of Dirichlet L-functions on the critical line.

Dynamics of the Bost–Connes State Space

We investigate whether the dynamics of the formation, motion, decay, and thermalization of matter in the physical world can be described within a unified and internally consistent mathematical framework. To this end, we start from the local decomposition of the Bost–Connes system and perform a finite p-adic reduction to construct the corresponding state space. Within this space, we characterize stable states relative to a background and establish a complete cycle consisting of free evolution, formation exchange, and pattern reconstruction. Excitation formation, motion, and decay, together with relaxation of the ensemble occupation distribution, are determined by the same joint evolution and its iterations, with the final state of each cycle serving as the initial state of the next. References, observation, and records are incorporated into the same joint construction, giving the evolution of a subject and its observable manifestations a common mathematical basis. Under the corresponding conditions, we derive event statistics, convergence to stationarity, and thermalization, and use joint conservation and entropy balances to relate reversible joint evolution to irreversible system relaxation.

The Mechanism of Thermalization in the Bost–Connes System

The Bost–Connes system supplies KMS equilibrium states, but the evaluation algebra and its inner fluctuations preserve every valuation shell, no dynamics within the existing structure carries a general initial state to equilibrium, and thermalization requires a cross-shell dilation. This paper supplies that dilation and proves its construction unique component by component. The mechanism of thermalization is repeated collisions between the system and the environment. Each collision performs an energy-conserving single-quantum exchange between an environment mode and the shell levels, and the system moves one step at a time along the valuation shells; the KMS occupation of the environment sets the ratio of ascent to descent rates to p^{−β}, the shell occupations approach the Gibbs distribution under detailed balance, and the weak-coupling limit converges to a GKSL semigroup; the photons carrying the records escape without return, so the relaxation is irreversible. At fixed temperature the relative entropy decreases monotonically toward equilibrium, and the only time-asymmetric assumption is the boundary condition of environment refreshing. In units of the total rate, the relaxation spectrum is completely determined by tree geometry and detailed balance, independent of the microscopic branch weights, and the infinite-depth spectral gap is the bound (√p−1)²/(p+1) permitted by regular-tree geometry. The Arrhenius temperature dependence of generation-label mixing and the Majorana phase criterion provide experimental tests.

The Mechanism of the Motion of Matter from the Bost–Connes System

The structure of matter and the mechanism of thermalization have been established on the state space of the Bost–Connes system; the motion of matter has so far had no definition. Starting from the hierarchy between the state space and observational spacetime, we define motion as the cycle-by-cycle displacement of the reconstruction center of a standing wave in the state space, which appears in observational spacetime as a sequence of records. Propagation proceeds in units of cycles, each cycle consisting of three steps, free evolution, formation exchange, and pattern reconstruction, and the exact dispersion relation follows; a record carries phase away through a really emitted photon, an event is the readout of a record, and time is the phase count carried by records. The conservation laws hold in the state space as operator identities and appear in observational spacetime as event-by-event balance; the representative phenomena of motion, double-slit interference, tunneling, entanglement, and macroscopic trajectories, are explained one by one from the same structure.

The Mechanism of Fields from the Bost–Connes System

The structure of the forces and the response of matter to a non-uniform background have been established on the state space of the Bost–Connes system; the field has so far had no definition. Starting from the definition of motion, we define a field as the region-by-region values of a background quantity in the state space; it appears in observational spacetime as the statistics of events. The standing-wave cycles of matter occupy the spectral weight of the region they inhabit and accumulate channel phase along dwell segments, and the corrections are redistributed outward conservatively; the deduction of occupation gives the gravitational potential, the metric, and the source equation, and the accumulation of phase gives the field equations of the electromagnetic, weak, nuclear, and color interactions. Following the duality of discrete and continuous, the equations are written on two levels, discrete structural equations in the state space and differential equations for statistical field quantities in observational spacetime, and the classical Maxwell equations hold closed under the statistical readout. The nonlinear equation of gravity agrees with general relativity at all orders of the existing tests, and the next-order coefficient of the spatial metric component gives a discriminable deviation. The representative puzzles of fields, action at a distance, the photoelectric effect, the localization of field energy, the graviton, and the classical limit, are resolved one by one from the same structure.

The Mechanism of Decay from the Bost–Connes System

The structure, thermalization, and motion of matter have been established on the state space of the Bost–Connes system; the change of identity has so far had no mechanism. We define decay as the change of identity of a stable standing wave, χj → χk, and it proceeds in only one way: the amplitude of the initial state merges into the background, the final state re-forms from the background, and an emitted pair carries the record away. Merging and formation are evaluated at the vertices against the background; the evaluation gives the conservation of electric charge, generation charge, and parity, the direction and rate of decay follow from it, and the Fermi constant and the muon lifetime are given by the numbers of the framework. The exponential decay law, the β spectrum, parity violation, the lifetime hierarchy, and nucleon stability are explained one by one from the same structure.

The Unification of Formation, Motion, Decay, and Thermalization of Matter from the Bost–Connes System

The formation, motion, decay, and thermalization of matter have each been established on the state space of the Bost–Connes system, but the complete cycle map has not yet been constructed. On the finite joint space taking part in the exchange, this paper composes free evolution, formation exchange, and pattern reconstruction into a unitary cycle, with the exchange matrix elements and the environment preparation given as conditions of the construction. A standing wave exchanges continuously with the environment during its propagation, and the system state at the end of the cycle and the outgoing records are determined by the same joint evolution; the environment is refreshed cycle by cycle, and the system continues to evolve under the same map. Retrieval, transit, and transport together determine the maintenance of the standing wave, the change of identity, and the formation of records, while the reverse exchange replenishes formation under the corresponding incident occupation. Record weights are determined, under explicit statistical conditions, by the squared norms of the components of the joint state, and energy and the corresponding conserved charges are balanced between system and environment. Under a specified coherent detection preparation, a conserving cycle realizes position records with controlled error and gives error bounds for the interference visibility; the corresponding finite model gives conditional thermalization during identity maintenance and the equilibrium of formation and exit. Under fixed supply and convergence conditions, the steady total occupation of a linear ensemble equals the product of the production rate and the mean waiting time to the first exit. The evolution of a single standing wave, the occupation of many standing waves, and the statistics of actual records are thereby connected within the same cycle.

BC State-Space Computer

That excitations are countable, that energy is additive, and that independent composition multiplies are three laboratory-level basic facts; their minimal algebraic form is the Bost–Connes (BC) covariance structure. Starting from this we define the BC state-space computer: a controllable physical system that carries the BC covariance relations in finite truncation within finite experimental boundaries. A run is the complete chain that takes external conditions through boundary formation, preparation, constrained evolution, stopping and readout, to return coupling; computation is defined on the run law by a task, a reusable boundary program, physical compilation, and completion criteria. Three structural dilemmas of the prevailing paradigm close at the physical-structure level of this machine. Content processing is carried directly by the state space, and step-by-step replay does not arise; statistics and thermalization are native resources, and pseudorandom reconstruction does not arise; the interface to the world is carried by the physical coupling of transduction and actuation, and symbolic replicas do not arise. The supporting construction consists of the arithmetic workspace (prime occupations carry multiplicative composition, phase sectors carry addition), machine parts organized into an arithmetic and an interaction module, and running instances that progress from elementary operations to sustained tracking. The thermalization mechanism is stated conditionally, all verdict quantities are pre-registered by a numerical twin, and whether devices physically realize them is left to independent experiments.

Abstract

Starting from a finite p-adic reduction of the Bost–Connes system, we investigate the common dynamical origin of matter, mass, and forces. Matter is characterized as stable excitations whose patterns are preserved under propagation and exchange: the relative phases of their persistent evolution yield mass frequencies, exchange between stable states produces resonance responses, and the influence of the background energy distribution on motion produces a metric response.

We construct the complete process from the formation of a stable pattern to its persistent evolution, bringing path spectral responses, node overlaps, and distributions across generations into a single mass generator. The same mass parameter then governs both the rest-frame energy gap and the inertial response at low momentum, while a common background response provides a unified energy scale.

On the basis of these dynamical relations, we use an odd prime p as a structural parameter to establish evaluation relations for mass spectra and coupling constants. With p=7 and the common energy calibration E0=1 GeV, we obtain an electroweak scale of 246.19 GeV and an inverse electromagnetic coupling of αEM−1=137.03636. The absolute relative deviations of the nine charged-fermion masses from the reference central values are all below 1.5%, and those of the three charged leptons are below 0.003%. The neutrino spectrum predicts absolute masses of 5.07, 9.96, and 50.63 meV for the three mass eigenstates, with a mass-squared difference ratio of Δm312/Δm212=34.52.
