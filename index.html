The Unification of Matter, Forces, and Spacetime from the Bost–Connes System
ShaoWu Hu · July 6, 2026
Preprint (V1)
Starting from the Bost–Connes system, we perform the p-adic reduction at a prime p and construct the state space H_p = L²(ℤ/p^Kℤ), K = p(p−1)−1, and argue that its structural properties naturally describe the physical world. By analyzing the structure of the state space, we derive general-p formulas for spacetime, matter, forces, and cosmic evolution; when the characteristic quantities of the p channels are compared with observation—three generations of fermions, five elementary particles, three gauge forces, and the Standard Model gauge group—the unique compatible value is p = 7: the physical world we inhabit is described by the state space over ℤ/7ℤ. Fixing p = 7 in the general-p formulas and computing the outputs one by one: the electroweak scale 246.19 GeV (−0.011%), the Planck mass 1.221×10¹⁹ GeV (+0.04%), the proton mass 937 MeV (−0.17%), the nine charged-fermion masses all within 1.6%, the electromagnetic coupling α_EM⁻¹ = 137.036 (+0.0003%), the color coupling α_s⁻¹ = 8.49 (+0.08%), the weak coupling α_W⁻¹ = 29.60 (−0.004%), the nuclear coupling α_nuclear = 14.10 (+0.7%), and the gravitational constant G = 6.70×10⁻³⁹ GeV⁻² (−0.09%). Neutrinos are of Majorana type, with absolute masses 5.07, 9.96, 50.6 meV for the three generations and mass-squared-difference ratio 34.5 (experiment 33.8±0.9), a decisive prediction.

We analyze, from the arithmetic structure of the Bost–Connes (BC) system, the correspondence of each of the five von Neumann axioms of quantum mechanics within the BC framework. The main results are as follows: (I) C*-positivity φ(a*a)≥0 naturally gives rise to the Hilbert space ℓ²(ℕ⁺); (II) arithmetic function operators commuting with H describe self-adjoint observables; (III) the overlap relation between KMS states encodes the Born rule P=|cₙ|²; (IV) the strict monotonicity of ζ(β) determines the unitary time evolution U(t)=e^{−itH}; (V) the fundamental theorem of arithmetic n=∏p^{aₚ} corresponds to the tensor product decomposition |n⟩=⊗|aₚ⟩.

Cosmology and the Bost–Connes System
ShaoWu Hu · April 15, 2026
Preprint (V10)
Starting from the Hamiltonian H = log n of the Bost–Connes system, this paper derives the complete chain of cosmological evolution using the inverse temperature β as the sole time coordinate. The unique pole of ζ(β) at β = 1 produces simultaneous divergences of the energy density, entropy, and number of states, yielding an arithmetic description of the Big Bang. For β > 1 a four-dimensional spacetime emerges from the discrete metric, together with the discrete gravitational equation −Δ(log n) = R(n) and the emergent gravitational constant G(N) = C*/(N log²N); the Planck mass is M_Pl = 1.221×10¹⁹ GeV (deviation +0.04%). Symmetry breaking at β = 1 endows all characters of (ℤ/7ℤ)* with distinct identities, giving rise to all particle species and mass hierarchies; the electroweak scale is v_EW = 247.6 GeV (deviation +0.56%). At β ∼ 10², the baryon asymmetry η = 6.12×10⁻¹⁰ (deviation 0.1%) and the baryon density Ω_b h² = 0.0224 (deviation 0.1%). At β ∼ 10¹⁷, the residue of the ζ-pole and the negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature yield Λ > 0, Ω_Λ = 0.685, and Λ = 2.85×10⁻¹²². The finiteness of the gravitational degrees of freedom N renders the heat death at β → ∞ reachable in finite time.

Arithmetic Structure of Baryons and the Bost–Connes System
ShaoWu Hu · May 22, 2026
Preprint (V5)
Of the proton mass 938 MeV, the three valence quarks contribute only about 1%; the remaining 99% non-constituent mass, the absolute stability of the proton (τ_p > 10³⁴ yr), color confinement, and the Regge regularity of baryon excitations have long lacked a unified analytic explanation. This paper proves that all of these phenomena are uniquely described by the Hamiltonian layer H = log n of the Bost–Connes spectral triple. The KMS partition function equals the Riemann ζ function; its unique pole at β = 1 is the arithmetic origin of color confinement and baryon emergence. A parameter-free mass formula E_n = √(ζ(β) log²ζ(β)/C*) with β = 1+1/√n gives E₃ = 937 MeV (−0.2%). Further predictions: n–p mass difference 1.29 MeV (−0.2%), μ_p/μ_n = −1.459 (0.06%), g_A = 1.311 (+2.8%), τ_n = 930 s (+4.8%), m_π = 137 MeV (−1.7%), nuclear force coupling α_nuclear = 14.1 (+0.8%). Eleven independent observables agree with experiment to ≲3% precision with zero adjustable parameters.

BC State-Space Computer
ShaoWu Hu · July 31, 2026
Preprint (V32)
That excitations are countable, that energy is additive, and that independent composition multiplies are three laboratory-level basic facts; their minimal algebraic form is the Bost–Connes (BC) covariance structure. Starting from this we define the BC state-space computer: a controllable physical system that carries the BC covariance relations in finite truncation within finite experimental boundaries. A run is the complete chain that takes external conditions through boundary formation, preparation, constrained evolution, stopping and readout, to return coupling; computation is defined on the run law by a task, a reusable boundary program, physical compilation, and completion criteria. Three structural dilemmas of the prevailing paradigm close at the physical-structure level of this machine. Content processing is carried directly by the state space, and step-by-step replay does not arise; statistics and thermalization are native resources, and pseudorandom reconstruction does not arise; the interface to the world is carried by the physical coupling of transduction and actuation, and symbolic replicas do not arise. The supporting construction consists of the arithmetic workspace (prime occupations carry multiplicative composition, phase sectors carry addition), machine parts organized into an arithmetic and an interaction module, and running instances that progress from elementary operations to sustained tracking. The thermalization mechanism is stated conditionally, all verdict quantities are pre-registered by a numerical twin, and whether devices physically realize them is left to independent experiments.
