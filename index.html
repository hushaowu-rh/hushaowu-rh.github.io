Cosmology and the Bost–Connes System
ShaoWu Hu · April 15, 2026
Preprint (V10)
Starting from the Hamiltonian H = log n of the Bost–Connes system, this paper derives the complete chain of cosmological evolution using the inverse temperature β as the sole time coordinate. The unique pole of ζ(β) at β = 1 produces simultaneous divergences of the energy density, entropy, and number of states, yielding an arithmetic description of the Big Bang. For β > 1 a four-dimensional spacetime emerges from the discrete metric, together with the discrete gravitational equation −Δ(log n) = R(n) and the emergent gravitational constant G(N) = C*/(N log²N); the Planck mass is M_Pl = 1.221×10¹⁹ GeV (deviation +0.04%). Symmetry breaking at β = 1 endows all characters of (ℤ/7ℤ)* with distinct identities, giving rise to all particle species and mass hierarchies; the electroweak scale is v_EW = 247.6 GeV (deviation +0.56%). At β ∼ 10², the baryon asymmetry η = 6.12×10⁻¹⁰ (deviation 0.1%) and the baryon density Ω_b h² = 0.0224 (deviation 0.1%). At β ∼ 10¹⁷, the residue of the ζ-pole and the negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature yield Λ > 0, Ω_Λ = 0.685, and Λ = 2.85×10⁻¹²². The finiteness of the gravitational degrees of freedom N renders the heat death at β → ∞ reachable in finite time.

Preprint (V5)
Of the proton mass 938 MeV, the three valence quarks contribute only about 1%; the remaining 99% non-constituent mass, the absolute stability of the proton (τ_p > 10³⁴ yr), color confinement, and the Regge regularity of baryon excitations have long lacked a unified analytic explanation. This paper proves that all of these phenomena are uniquely described by the Hamiltonian layer H = log n of the Bost–Connes spectral triple. The KMS partition function equals the Riemann ζ function; its unique pole at β = 1 is the arithmetic origin of color confinement and baryon emergence. A parameter-free mass formula E_n = √(ζ(β) log²ζ(β)/C*) with β = 1+1/√n gives E₃ = 937 MeV (−0.2%). Eleven independent observables agree with experiment to ≲3% precision with zero adjustable parameters.

Unification of Matter and Forces: Bost–Connes Spectral Dynamics
ShaoWu Hu · June 17, 2026
Preprint (V4)
This series of works, starting from the Bost–Connes system, has separately established arithmetic descriptions of Standard Model fermion masses, gauge groups and coupling constants, baryon spectra, and cosmological scales. These results repeatedly use the same set of mathematical ingredients—the Hamiltonian H = log n, the character group (ℤ/7ℤ)* and its L-functions, KMS states and the β = 1 phase transition—but have never been organized as a whole. This paper unifies these ingredients into the arithmetic Dirac operator D = H⊗I_F + γ⊗D_F, and develops BC spectral dynamics along the logical chain: matter → forces → coupling strengths. Matter consists of the spectral eigenmodes of D: the angular part corresponds to internal quantum numbers and identity, while the radial part corresponds to spatial and energy scales. Forces arise as Green-kernel responses: angular L-functions propagate gauge forces, the radial ζ-function produces gravity, and the angular×radial combination gives nuclear forces. Coupling constants are uniformly written as α_X = |A_X|²η_X, with the distinction lying in the source of the vertex amplitude (L-function or curvature constant C*) and the statistical treatment of internal channels (averaging or summation).
